Which clock does an autonomous adversary actually move?

A Monte Carlo model of exploit availability against remediation, resimulated on every change. The adversary timeline is measured, not assumed: days from CVE publication to public exploit code. That clock has sat at its floor — a median under one day — in every settled year since 2015. Three other mechanisms have not: weaponised share, post-exploitation tempo, and vulnerability discovery rate.

01

Exploit availability against remediation

Day zero is patch availability. Everything to the left of it is exploitation that precedes any available fix. The hatched region is the probability that public exploit code exists before remediation completes.

remediation complete exploit available public before the CVE record unremediated exposure

How to read this chart

The horizontal axis is days from CVE publication. Day 0 is the vertical rule, and the shaded band to its left is time when no CVE record existed yet. Most of that band is not a zero-day: it is publication lag, with exploit code already public and mass scanning already following it, which is why the model gives it full hazard rather than the discount a genuinely pre-disclosure exploit gets. The same measured series reports the share negative at 98.5% in 2000, on medians of tens of days before publication — impossible as a claim about adversaries, unremarkable as one about NVD.

Two distributions mirror off the centre line. Above it, remediation complete is when your remediation lands, day by day. Below it, exploit available is when public exploit code appears, drawn twice: as a faint daily density, and as the bold cumulative curve. The cumulative curve reads against its own scale, which runs from 0% at the centre line down to 100% at the bottom.

Unremediated exposure is the hatched region. For each day it is the part of that day’s remediation for which exploit code already existed, so it is the share of the remediation curve that lost the race.

The two large figures answer different questions. The marker on the cumulative curve at day 0 is the share of exploits that are already public when the CVE record lands. The badge is the share that arrive before your remediation completes, which is always the later test of the two and therefore always the larger number. The percentages at the edges are exploit or remediation mass falling outside the drawn window, labelled there rather than folded into the end bins, which would invent a spike that is not in the data.

02

Attrition from publication to compromise

Each stage is a subset of the one above it. The stream entering the top is every severity band, not just Critical: you set the critical count and the model derives the High, Medium and Low volume beside it from the published corpus ratios, each carrying its own arming and exploitation rate. Counting criticals alone would discard two thirds of everything known to be exploited. The two largest reductions then occur outside the remediation process — most published vulnerabilities land on a version or configuration that is not exposed to them, and most of the remainder never acquire a working exploit.

03

Sensitivity analysis

Each bar varies a single parameter across its plausible range with all others held constant. A compromise is an adversary establishing a foothold; it becomes an incident when detection and response fail to contain it before the adversary reaches its objective. The two respond to different parameters, and that divergence is what should drive remediation priority. Read the ranking rather than the ratio between bars: length depends on the range chosen for each parameter. Detection sits flat against compromise by construction, being modelled only after a foothold exists, so controls that block exploitation outright are outside the model rather than measured at zero. Headcount is a scale term, not a lever: the routes that scale with people do so sub-linearly, because one lure reaches every mailbox in a single event, and a bar that long would otherwise read as advice to employ fewer people.

    07

    Four clocks an autonomous adversary could move

    BOD 26-04 names adversary use of AI as a reason the window between patch release and exploitation may narrow. That is four separate claims carrying different evidence and different consequences, so each takes its own dial on one axis. is the one the phrase usually means, and the flattest curve here: the measured median has not exceeded one day in any settled year since 2015, and the share arriving ahead of the CVE record has fallen rather than risen. is worth several times as much, because breadth has room left where speed does not. is flat here by construction: speed from foothold to objective cannot change whether you were breached, only whether anyone reached it in time. Switch the readout to incidents and it lifts off the floor. Even there the curve understates where it bites: against a 24/7 SOC, full tempo removes roughly half of the margin detection buys over having none. A faster adversary does not beat detection on any single intrusion; it devalues the investment.

    The steepest curve here is the last one, and it is not a clock at all. asks how many vulnerabilities are found in software you already run: the size of the stream rather than the speed of anything that happens to it. Machine-found bugs in real codebases are a present-tense capability rather than a forecast, which makes this the least speculative of the four and, on most estates, comfortably the largest. All four dials default to the measured record.

    08

    Why severity is not the prioritisation trigger

    The conventional approach counts Critical-rated CVEs. The corpus does not support it. Below is the full scored CVE population, bucketed by CVSS band, against the share of each band that has reached the confirmed-exploited catalogue.

    CVSS bandScored CVEsConfirmed exploitedRate

    This position is now codified. CISA Binding Operational Directive 26-04 (10 June 2026) supersedes BOD 22-01 and removes severity as the trigger entirely. It prioritises on four SSVC decision points: presence in the KEV catalogue, public exposure of the asset, whether exploitation is automatable, and the technical impact obtained. Remediation tiers run from three days plus forensic triage down to “fix on system upgrade”. The published heuristic for automatable is a public proof-of-concept achieving remote code execution that executes reliably, which is the same primitive this model measures.

    09

    Severity-label inflation

    The Critical-rated population is growing faster than the corpus that contains it. A model anchored to criticals per year therefore drifts more pessimistic each year for reasons unrelated to the estate being assessed. This is the second reason severity is not used as an input here.

    Part of that increase reflects scoring policy rather than vulnerability reality. CNAs self-assign scores, and the share each rates 9.0 or above ranges from near zero to roughly two in five against the same scale. NVD has also ceased analysis on a growing share of the corpus.

    10

    Model provenance

    Three classes of coefficient, distinguished rather than blended. measured is read directly from the vendored corpus and is reproducible offline. reported is a dated published figure: cited, but dependent on a third party's methodology. assumed is judgement, used where no public measurement exists. The latter two are drawn from their stated range on every block of trials, which is the source of the credible bands. Where a coefficient is contested, the stated range is the parameter to challenge.

    Method

    60,000 simulated years across 650 coefficient blocks per parameter change, dropping to 4,000 years while a control is being dragged. The adversary timeline is sampled from the measured distribution of days between CVE publication and public exploit code. The remediation timeline is derived from triage delay, out-of-band capability and routine cadence. Compromise is modelled as a correlated campaign arrival rather than as independent per-system trials, which would saturate near 100% and overstate the result.

    Provenance

    Vulnerability, exploit-catalogue, EPSS and KEV figures are from the CyberMon snapshot …, vendored under data/cybermon/ so every number here is reproducible offline. Regenerate with node tools/refresh-data.js && node tools/derive-calibration.js. Verify the model with node test/model.test.js.

    Scope

    The classes counted and excluded are listed beside the headline figure. Credential abuse has led the DBIR initial-access ranking throughout the series, ahead of vulnerability exploitation. A model built on the patch clock alone therefore misses the larger half of the routes, which is why the classes needing no vulnerability are simulated here rather than left as a residual rate.

    Every coefficient behind those classes is judgement rather than measurement: no public corpus answers them the way the CVE corpus answers the exploit clock. What is checkable is the mix, which at the baseline estate is anchored to the reported initial-access distribution, and a test fails if it drifts. … Run-rate figures are linear extrapolation from a partial year.

    Sources for the reported tier

    Breakout timing from the CrowdStrike Global Threat Report 2026 (29-minute average eCrime breakout, fastest observed 27 seconds); dwell and notification-path timings from Mandiant M-Trends 2026 (14-day global median, 10 days when an organisation finds it itself against 26 when somebody else tells them); remediation velocity against the Verizon DBIR 2026 edge-device series (a median around a month to full remediation, with roughly half never fully remediated) and the Edgescan and Cyentia vulnerability-management series; containment bounded by Sophos State of Ransomware 2026 (44% of attacks stopped before encryption — a per-attack rate, so the quantity read against it is containment per intrusion rather than the chance every intrusion in a year was contained).

    These are vendor incident-response and survey populations, which skew toward organisations that needed help, and none of them is reproducible from the data vendored in this repository the way the CVE corpus is. They are cited and dated so a reader can go and look; the coefficients drawn from them carry wide declared ranges, and none of them is load-bearing on the ordering this page argues for. Everything in the measured tier is regenerated from the snapshot and checked in CI.
