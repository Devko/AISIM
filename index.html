    07

    Three clocks an autonomous adversary could move

    BOD 26-04 names adversary use of AI as a reason the window between patch release and exploitation may narrow. That is three separate claims carrying different evidence and different consequences, so each takes its own dial on one axis. Arrival speed is the one the phrase usually means, and the flattest curve here: the measured median has not exceeded one day in any settled year since 2015, and the pre-disclosure share has fallen rather than risen. Weaponised share is worth roughly three times as much, because breadth has room left where speed does not. Post-exploitation tempo is flat on this metric, exactly flat, because speed from foothold to objective cannot change whether you were breached, only whether anyone reached it in time. Switch the readout to incidents and it lifts off the floor. Even there the curve understates where it bites: against a 24/7 SOC, full tempo removes roughly three quarters of the margin detection buys over having none, taking it from 9.3 points of incident rate to 2.2. A faster adversary does not beat detection on any single intrusion; it devalues the investment. All three dials default to the measured record.

    08

    Why severity is not the prioritisation trigger

    The conventional approach counts Critical-rated CVEs. The corpus does not support it. Below is the full scored CVE population, bucketed by CVSS band, against the share of each band that has reached the confirmed-exploited catalogue.

    CVSS bandScored CVEsConfirmed exploitedRate

    This position is now codified. CISA Binding Operational Directive 26-04 (10 June 2026) supersedes BOD 22-01 and removes severity as the trigger entirely. It prioritises on four SSVC decision points: presence in the KEV catalogue, public exposure of the asset, whether exploitation is automatable, and the technical impact obtained. Remediation tiers run from three days plus forensic triage down to “fix on system upgrade”. The published heuristic for automatable is a public proof-of-concept achieving remote code execution that executes reliably, which is the same primitive this model measures.

    09

    Severity-label inflation

    The Critical-rated population is growing faster than the corpus that contains it. A model anchored to criticals per year therefore drifts more pessimistic each year for reasons unrelated to the estate being assessed. This is the second reason severity is not used as an input here.

    Part of that increase reflects scoring policy rather than vulnerability reality. CNAs self-assign scores, and the share each rates 9.0 or above ranges from near zero to roughly two in five against the same scale. NVD has also ceased analysis on a growing share of the corpus.

    10

    Model provenance

    Three classes of coefficient, distinguished rather than blended. measured is read directly from the vendored corpus and is reproducible offline. reported is a dated published figure: cited, but dependent on a third party's methodology. assumed is judgement, used where no public measurement exists. The latter two are drawn from their stated range on every block of trials, which is the source of the credible bands. Where a coefficient is contested, the stated range is the parameter to challenge.

    Method

    60,000 simulated years across 150 blocks per parameter change, with a 4,000-year pass while a control is moving. The adversary timeline is sampled from the measured distribution of days between CVE publication and public exploit code. The remediation timeline is derived from triage delay, out-of-band capability and routine cadence. Compromise is modelled as a correlated campaign arrival rather than as independent per-system trials, since independent draws saturate at 100% and misstate the result.

    Provenance

    Vulnerability, exploit-catalogue, EPSS and KEV figures are from the CyberMon snapshot …, vendored under data/cybermon/ so every number here is reproducible offline. Regenerate with node tools/refresh-data.js && node tools/derive-calibration.js. Verify the model with node test/model.test.js.

    Scope

    Three access routes are simulated: opportunistic exploitation, targeted campaign and supply chain. Not simulated: phishing; credential abuse; insider action. Every probability here is therefore a lower bound on intrusion risk rather than a complete picture of it, which is why the coverage statement sits beside the headline and on the routes chart rather than only here. The 2026 DBIR attributes 31% of breaches to vulnerability exploitation, the largest single initial-access category and still a minority of the total. The one place the absent routes appear at all is campaign success without a vulnerability, a residual rate rather than a simulation of them; the sensitivity chart ranks it first of seventeen on the compromise metric, which is the honest way to report a proxy that load-bearing. Run-rate figures are linear extrapolation from a partial year.

    Sources for the reported tier

    Breakout timing from the CrowdStrike Global Threat Report 2026 (29-minute average eCrime breakout, fastest observed 27 seconds); dwell and notification-path timings from Mandiant M-Trends 2026 (14-day global median, 10 days when an organisation finds it itself against 26 when somebody else tells them); containment corroborated against Sophos State of Ransomware 2026 (44% of attacks stopped before encryption). These are vendor incident-response populations, which skew toward organisations that required incident response. Read them as the shape of the distribution rather than as a population baseline.
